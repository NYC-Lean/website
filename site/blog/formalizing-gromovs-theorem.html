Formalizing Gromov's Theorem in Lean

A talk by Aaron Hill.

August 28, 2026

This Sunday we're hosting Aaron Hill to present his Lean formalization of Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth. From his abstract:

This talk will present a Lean formalization of the forward direction of Gromov's polynomial growth theorem: every finitely generated group of polynomial growth is virtually nilpotent.

— Aaron Hill, Formalizing Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth in Lean

Fix a finite generating set for a group and count how many elements can be written as words of length at most n; the group has polynomial growth when that count is bounded by a polynomial in n. Gromov's theorem, proved in 1981, says this coarse geometric condition forces genuine algebraic structure: any such group contains a nilpotent subgroup of finite index. Together with the earlier converse of Wolf and the growth estimates of Bass and Guivarc'h, it shows that polynomial growth characterizes the virtually nilpotent groups exactly — a founding result of geometric group theory, where properties of a group are read off from the large-scale geometry of its Cayley graph.

It is also a serious thing to formalize. The known proofs all pass through heavy machinery — Gromov's original argument views the group from far away, taking a limit of rescaled Cayley graphs and invoking the structure theory of locally compact groups behind Hilbert's fifth problem, while Kleiner's 2007 proof runs instead through finite-dimensional spaces of harmonic functions. Come hear which route the formalization takes, and how the pieces fit together in Lean, at his talk this Sunday, August 30.
