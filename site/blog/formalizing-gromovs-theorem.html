Formalizing Gromov's Theorem in Lean

A talk by Aaron Hill.

August 28, 2026

This Sunday we're hosting Aaron Hill to present his Lean formalization of Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth. Proved in 1981, the theorem draws a strong structural conclusion from nothing more than how quickly a group grows. The original paper states it in one line:

If a finitely generated group Γ has polynomial growth then Γ contains a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.

— Mikhael Gromov, Groups of polynomial growth and expanding maps, Publ. Math. IHÉS 53 (1981)

Together with the earlier converse of Wolf and the growth estimates of Bass and Guivarc'h, this shows that polynomial growth characterizes the virtually nilpotent groups exactly — a founding result of geometric group theory, where properties of a group are read off from the large-scale geometry of its Cayley graph.

It is also a serious thing to formalize, whichever proof you follow. As Terence Tao put it when he wrote up Kleiner's proof on his blog:

Gromov's original argument used a number of deep tools, including the Montgomery-Zippin-Yamabe structure theory of locally compact groups (related to Hilbert's fifth problem), as well as various earlier partial results on the problem. Several proofs have subsequently been found. Recently, Kleiner obtained a proof which was significantly more elementary, although it still relies on some non-trivial partial versions of Gromov's theorem.

— Terence Tao, Kleiner's proof of Gromov's theorem, What's new

Come hear which route the formalization takes, and how the pieces fit together in Lean, at his talk this Sunday, August 30.
