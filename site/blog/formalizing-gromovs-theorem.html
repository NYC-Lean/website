Formalizing Gromov's Theorem in Lean

A talk by Aaron Hill.

August 28, 2026

This Sunday we're hosting Aaron Hill to present his Lean formalization of Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth. Proved in 1981, the theorem gives information about the global structure of a group based only on the behavior of a finite set of generators. The original paper states it in one line:

If a finitely generated group Γ has polynomial growth then Γ contains a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.

— Mikhael Gromov, Groups of polynomial growth and expanding maps, Publ. Math. IHÉS 53 (1981)

Gromov builds on earlier work of Wolf, Bass, and Guivarc'h, who by 1973 had proved the converse: a nilpotent subgroup of finite index implies polynomial growth. The theorem is a founding result of geometric group theory, where it finds applications in the classification of expanding maps, the analysis of random walks, and the topology of Riemannian manifolds.

Aaron's formalization follows the proof Terence Tao presented on his blog in 2010:

The argument requires four separate ingredients. The first is the existence of non-trivial Lipschitz harmonic functions… The second is that there are not too many such harmonic functions… The third ingredient is that Gromov's theorem is true in the compact linear Lie group case… The final ingredient is that Gromov's theorem is inductively true once one can locate an infinite cyclic quotient.

— Terence Tao, A proof of Gromov's theorem, What's new

The development itself runs to some 28,000 lines of Lean over Mathlib, its files tracking the skeleton of Tao's argument — the Poincaré inequality, the packing argument, the finite-dimensionality of the space of Lipschitz harmonic functions — down to a main theorem the code itself checks to be free of sorries and stray axioms. It even cross-checks its statement against the independent one in DeepMind's Formal Conjectures library, insurance against proving the wrong theorem.

Come hear how the pieces fit together in Lean at his talk this Sunday, August 30.
